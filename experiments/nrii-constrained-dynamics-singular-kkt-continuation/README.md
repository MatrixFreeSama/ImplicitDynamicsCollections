# NRII Constrained-Dynamics Singular-KKT Continuation

## Status

Literature-grounded benchmark adaptation. This is **not** a bitwise reproduction of the numerical setup in any cited paper.

The benchmark isolates one question:

> Can a smooth physical trajectory continue through a manipulator singular configuration when a conventional full-rank KKT/Newton formulation loses numerical rank, while a Matrix-Free NRII state construction does not depend on that KKT solve?

The answer for the locked test below is yes, with an important limitation: an SVD pseudoinverse specialist also resolves the rank-deficient linear subproblem. The result therefore supports a **formulation-bypass / solver-handoff** claim, not a claim that all established methods fail.

## Literature basis

Two published observations motivate the test.

1. S. K. Ider and F. M. L. Amirouche, “Numerical stability of the constraints near singular positions in the dynamics of multibody systems,” *Computers & Structures*, 33(1), 129–137, 1989. DOI: `10.1016/0045-7949(89)90135-1`.

   The paper states that constraint equations may become linearly dependent at instantaneous singular configurations, producing singularities in conventional multibody simulation algorithms, and illustrates the issue with a three-link planar manipulator.

2. N. C. Parida and S. Raha, “Regularized numerical integration of multibody dynamics with the generalized alpha method,” *Applied Mathematics and Computation*, 215(3), 1224–1243, 2009. DOI: `10.1016/j.amc.2009.06.063`.

   The paper analyzes singular/high-index multibody DAEs and ill-conditioned discretization Jacobians, including singular configurations, and studies regularization by increasing the smallest singular values of the Jacobian.

This repository benchmark uses the same **class of pathology** but its own reproducible numerical model.

## Mechanical core

A three-link planar serial manipulator is used as the singularity-producing core.

Link data:

- lengths: `(1.0, 0.8, 0.6)`
- masses: `(1.2, 1.0, 0.8)`
- uniform-rod centroidal inertias

The end-effector position Jacobian is

\[
J(q)=\frac{\partial r_E}{\partial q}.
\]

When

\[
q_2=q_3=0,
\]

all three links are collinear. Every instantaneous end-effector velocity generated by a joint rotation is perpendicular to that same line, so the two Cartesian rows of \(J\) become linearly dependent:

\[
\operatorname{rank}J:2\rightarrow1.
\]

## Dense lifted dynamics

To avoid a tiny, specially structured matrix, the 3-DOF mechanical core is embedded into a 24-coordinate system.

- physical manipulator coordinates: 3
- auxiliary smooth coordinates: 21
- total generalized coordinates: 24
- acceleration constraints: 10
- dense KKT dimension: \(34\times34\)

A fixed orthogonal mixing maps the physical/auxiliary coordinates into dense generalized coordinates. The auxiliary generalized masses span four decades. The resulting mass and constraint blocks are dense.

The acceleration-level constrained system is

\[
\begin{bmatrix}
M & G^T\\
G & 0
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
a\\
\lambda
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
Ma_*\\
Ga_*
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

The right-hand side is deliberately consistent with the smooth physical acceleration \(a_*\). Therefore at the singular configuration the physical acceleration remains available, while the constraint multiplier loses uniqueness.

This distinction is essential:

\[
\text{rank-deficient KKT}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{physical state is necessarily undefined}.
\]

## Smooth physical trajectory

The smooth physical coordinates are uncoupled oscillators. The second and third manipulator joint coordinates share the same frequency and are initialized so that the **discrete implicit-midpoint trajectory** reaches

\[
q_2=q_3=0
\]

at exactly step 200.

Locked integration:

- \(\Delta t=0.0025\)
- singular step: 200
- singular time: \(t=0.5\)
- target step: 400
- target time: \(t=1.0\)
- NRII order: 16

## Matrix-Free NRII path

For the smooth first-order state \(y=[u,v]\),

\[
\dot y=Ay,
\]

the implicit midpoint relation is

\[
y_{n+1}
=
y_n+\Delta t\,A\frac{y_n+y_{n+1}}2.
\]

NRII uses the coefficient construction

\[
C_1=\Delta t\,Ay_n,
\qquad
C_k=\frac{\Delta t}{2}AC_{k-1},
\quad k\ge2.
\]

Only the operator action \(Ay\) is evaluated. The dense KKT matrix is never assembled or solved in the NRII time-advance path.

The residual is audited only after candidate construction and never generates a correction inside NRII.

## Locked result

The full-rank Newton/KKT baseline uses the conventional requirement that its correction Jacobian be numerically full rank.

It advances through step 199:

\[
t=0.4975.
\]

At attempted step 200:

\[
t=0.5,
\]

the KKT diagnostics are

\[
\operatorname{rank}=33
\quad\text{for a }34\times34\text{ system},
\]

\[
\sigma_{\min}\approx5.50\times10^{-17},
\]

\[
\kappa_2\approx1.82\times10^{18}.
\]

The full-rank Newton/KKT continuation therefore stops with

`rank_deficient_KKT_Jacobian`.

The Matrix-Free NRII path does not request that solve and continues through the singular configuration to the declared target:

\[
t=1.0,
\qquad
400/400\text{ steps committed}.
\]

Across the complete run:

\[
\max\|R_{\mathrm{NRII}}\|_\infty
\approx2.56\times10^{-16},
\]

and the maximum absolute difference from an independent elementwise closed-form solve of the same implicit-midpoint map is

\[
1.94\times10^{-14}.
\]

## Important raw-LU control

At the rank-deficient step, `numpy.linalg.solve` may still return a floating-point vector because finite-precision roundoff perturbs the exact algebraic singularity.

In the locked run, raw LU returned a vector with a tiny backward residual. This is **not** treated as proof of a unique Newton correction because the SVD rank test reports 33 rather than 34.

This is intentionally analogous to the general numerical-analysis warning that a small residual alone does not certify a well-posed or unique state.

## SVD pseudoinverse control

A pseudoinverse solve is included at every step.

At the singular step it recovers the physical acceleration to approximately

\[
8.27\times10^{-14}
\]

relative forward error, with near-zero multiplier norm.

Therefore the conclusion is deliberately narrow:

> A full-rank KKT/Newton formulation can lose its continuation contract at a singular configuration even though the physical state can continue. Matrix-Free NRII can bypass that particular matrix dependency. This does not mean that SVD, projection, regularization, null-space, or other singularity-aware methods are unable to continue.

## Singularity sweep

`SINGULARITY_SWEEP.csv` independently moves

\[
q_2\rightarrow0,
\qquad q_3=0.6q_2,
\]

and records the task-Jacobian singular values, KKT smallest singular value, condition number, numerical rank, direct-solve forward/backward errors, multiplier norm, and pseudoinverse control.

The sweep includes a \(10^{-14}\)-relative perturbation of one constraint right-hand side to show sensitivity as the KKT system approaches rank loss.

## Claim boundary

Supported by this benchmark:

> In this locked dense constrained-dynamics adaptation, a full-rank Newton/KKT continuation contract stops at a rank-deficient manipulator singular configuration, while the Matrix-Free NRII implicit-midpoint path continues the same smooth physical state to the target time without assembling or solving that KKT system.

Not supported:

- that every Newton implementation fails at manipulator singularities;
- that NRII resolves physically underdetermined singularities without an additional selection rule;
- that NRII is faster than specialized projection, null-space, pseudoinverse, or regularized multibody methods;
- that this is an exact numerical reproduction of the 1989 or 2009 paper.
